Baths, shower trays & wet areas

A slippery bath doesn't need replacing. It needs treating.

Enamel baths and acrylic shower trays are among the most common slip risks in a hotel bedroom — and the most disruptive to put right by ripping them out. We make them slip-resistant where they are, in about 20 minutes a bath, and the room can be used straight afterwards.

Why treatment beats replacement

Replacing a bath or a shower tray means a room out of action, a plumber, a tiler and a bill. An anti-slip treatment works on the surface itself, so the fitting stays exactly where it is — and the room goes back into service instead of into a refurbishment schedule.

  • The room keeps earning. No strip-out, no re-tiling, no waiting on trades.
  • Around 20 minutes to treat a bath, so several bedrooms can be done in a single visit.
  • Baths and trays can be used immediately afterwards — enamel and acrylic alike. No cure time, no room standing empty overnight.
  • Whole-estate rollouts. Multiple rooms, or multiple sites, planned as one programme.
  • It still looks like your bathroom. The treatment works on the surface's microscopic texture, so appearance is usually unchanged.
  • Out-of-hours work available, so bedrooms can be done around your bookings.

The cost of getting it wrong

Don't become a slip-injury statistic.

Under Regulation 12 of the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992, your floors must be suitable for use — free from anything, including slipperiness, that puts people at risk. Slips and trips are the single most common cause of non-fatal workplace injury in Britain, and every incident carries real cost in compensation, lost time and reputation. A treated, tested floor removes that risk quickly and affordably.

Pendulum slip testing

The quickest, most trusted proof your floor is safe.

The pendulum test is the HSE's preferred method for measuring slip resistance, because it works in the wet conditions where slips actually happen. We test in dry and wet conditions, in three directions, to BS EN 16165:2021 (Annex C) and the UKSRG Guidelines, Issue 6 (Jan 2024) — on-site or from samples sent to our lab.

Book a pendulum test
High slip potential0 – 24
Moderate slip potential25 – 35
Low slip potential36+

Pendulum Test Value (PTV). After treatment we target a PTV of 36+ (low slip potential) in the relevant conditions and issue a Certificate of Conformity as your paper trail of due diligence. PTV is assessed as part of a wider risk assessment, not a simple pass/fail.

The 36 threshold traces back to Building Research Station studies in the late 1950s, still the basis of UK slip-risk assessment today.
